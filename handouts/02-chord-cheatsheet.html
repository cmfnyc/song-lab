Chord Cheatsheet

Roman numerals in 30 seconds

Chords get numbers based on their position in the key. The numbers let you move any pattern into any key.

  • UPPERCASE = major chord (bright)
  • lowercase = minor chord (darker)
Number I ii iii IV V vi
Key of C (home key) C Dm Em F G Am
Key of G (optional) G Am Bm C D Em

The I chord is home. Songs feel finished when they land there.

(There is also a vii° chord — Bdim in C — but pop songs rarely use it. Skip it.)

Ukulele shapes

Four numbers = the four strings, top to bottom: G C E A. 0 = open string.

Key of C

  • C = 0003
  • Dm = 2210
  • Em = 0432
  • F = 2010
  • G = 0232
  • Am = 2000

Key of G

  • G = 0232
  • Am = 2000
  • Bm = 4222 (harder)
  • C = 0003
  • D = 2220
  • Em = 0432

Strum patterns, melody notes, and Phase 2 chords are in Ukulele Songwriter's Guide.

What's in each chord (letter names)

You need these for the chord chart beside おんぷノート, and for playing chords on a GarageBand keyboard. A chord is three notes: skip a letter, take one, skip, take one.

Key of C

  • C = C E G
  • Dm = D F A
  • Em = E G B
  • F = F A C
  • G = G B D
  • Am = A C E

Key of G

  • G = G B D
  • Am = A C E
  • Bm = B D F♯
  • C = C E G
  • D = D F♯ A
  • Em = E G B

On a GarageBand keyboard: right hand plays the three notes, left hand plays just the chord's letter name, low.

Famous four-chord loops

Try each one. Write a mood word next to it.

  1. I – V – vi – IV — C G Am F / G D Em C — Mood: __________
  2. vi – IV – I – V — Am F C G / Em C G D — Mood: __________
  3. I – vi – IV – V — C Am F G / G Em C D — Mood: __________
  4. IV – I – V – vi — F C G Am / C G D Em — Mood: __________
  5. I – IV – vi – V — C F Am G / G C Em D — Mood: __________

Make your own: pick any four chords from I, ii, iii, IV, V, vi. Rules of thumb:

  • Starting on I feels settled and bright.
  • Starting on vi feels emotional.
  • Ending on V makes you want to go back to the start. Great for loops.
