Ukulele Songwriter's Guide

The ukulele is a great songwriting instrument. The most famous pop progression, C – G – Am – F, uses four of the easiest chords on it.

Your home key: C. Use the key of C for Song 1. The key of G also works well on ukulele if a song sits better for your voice there.

How to read the chord shapes

Four numbers, one for each string, in the order you hold the ukulele: G – C – E – A (top string to bottom string).

  • 0 = open string (no finger)
  • 2 = press at the 2nd fret

Example: Am = 2000 → 2nd fret on the G string, everything else open.

G V 0232 easy
Am vi 2000 easy

The hit-song loop: C (0003) → G (0232) → Am (2000) → F (2010)

Smooth-change tip: going from Am (2000) to F (2010), keep your 2nd-fret finger down and just add one finger. Look for fingers that can stay put when you change chords.

Phase 2 chords (Session 7)

In the key of C:

  • Fm (iv, bittersweet) = 1013
  • B♭ (♭VII, triumphant) = 3211 (1st-fret barre across the bottom two strings)
  • A♭ (♭VI, cinematic) = 1343
  • E (III, pulls to Am) = 1402, or the easier E7 = 1202

In the key of G:

  • Cm (iv) = 0333
  • F (♭VII) = 2010
  • E♭ (♭VI) = 0331
  • B7 (instead of B, pulls to Em) = 2322

Color chords (smooth, lo-fi sound): Am7 = 0000 (just strum the open strings), Cmaj7 = 0002, Dm7 = 2213, Fmaj7 = 2410

Strum patterns = chord rhythm

These match the chord rhythms in Song Lab and the Beat Patterns handout. D = down strum, U = up strum, X = chuck.

1. Whole notes (ballad)

Count:  1   &   2   &   3   &   4   &
Strum:  D   .   .   .   .   .   .   .

2. Quarters (driving)

Count:  1   &   2   &   3   &   4   &
Strum:  D   .   D   .   D   .   D   .

3. The push (modern pop)

Count:  1   &   2   &   3   &   4   &
Strum:  D   .   .   D   .   .   .   .

Strum on 1, then again on the "&" of 2, and let it ring.

4. Island strum (classic ukulele)

Count:  1   &   2   &   3   &   4   &
Strum:  D   .   D   U   .   U   D   U

Keep your hand moving down-up the whole time, even when you miss the strings.

5. The chuck (the ukulele's built-in snare drum)

Count:  1   &   2   &   3   &   4   &
Strum:  D   .   X   .   D   U   X   .

How to chuck: strum down and land the side of your palm on the strings right away, muting them with a percussive "chk." Putting the chuck on beats 2 and 4 is exactly where the snare goes. It makes a solo ukulele sound like a band.

Finding melody notes on the ukulele

The ukulele's top G string is tuned high (called "re-entrant" tuning), so the lowest note is the open C string: middle C. That means ukulele melodies sit right on the treble staff, perfect for おんぷノート.

Notes of the C major scale, first five frets:

  • C string: open = C (middle C) · 2 = D · 4 = E · 5 = F
  • E string: open = E · 1 = F · 3 = G
  • A string: open = A · 2 = B · 3 = C · 5 = D
  • G string (high G): open = G · 2 = A · 4 = B · 5 = C

Scale path, low to high: C string 0, 2, 4 → E string 1, 3 → A string 0, 2, 3 = C D E F G A B C

Hum a note, then hunt for it on this path. Write down the letter name, then put it in おんぷノート.

What the ukulele can't do: bass

Because of the high G string, the ukulele has no low notes. For basslines (Session 8):

  1. Find the bass note's letter name on the ukulele (the chord's root).
  2. Play it on a GarageBand Bass or Keyboard track, down low.
  3. Or use GarageBand's Smart Bass and tap the chord strips.

Recording your ukulele in GarageBand

A real ukulele track makes the song sound like you.

  1. Tune first. GarageBand has a built-in tuner, or use a free tuner app.
  2. Add an Audio Recorder track.
  3. Headphones on, metronome on.
  4. Mic placement: about 15–20 cm away, pointed at where the neck meets the body (not straight into the sound hole, which sounds boomy).
  5. Record 3 takes. Keep the steadiest one.
  6. Mistake in one bar? Record just that section again.

Ukulele as the lead: don't want to sing? Pluck the melody on the ukulele and record it as the lead track.

Ukulele songwriting tricks

  • Capo (if you have one): clip it on the 2nd fret and play the same C-shapes. The song moves up to the key of D, handy if the melody is too low for your voice.
  • Riff from a chord: hold a chord shape and pluck its strings one at a time in a pattern. Instant intro riff.
  • Stop-time: in the pre-chorus, strum once per bar and mute. Then burst into the full strum on the chorus.
  • Two ukulele parts: record strummed chords, then a second track of plucked notes. Pan them slightly apart.
