Bridge, Lift & Production Tricks

The bridge: something different

By the bridge, the listener has heard the verse and chorus twice. Give them something new, then send them back to the chorus fresh.

Pick two or three kinds of contrast:

  • New chords — start on a chord the song hasn't started on (IV or vi is common)
  • New melody shape — lower and wordier, or higher and longer, than the chorus
  • New lyric angle — a twist, a realization, the other side of the story
  • New energy — drop way down, or build way up

End the bridge on V (G in C, D in G). It pulls strongly back to the chorus.

Bridge chord ideas (key of C):

  • F – G – Am – Am (IV – V – vi – vi)
  • Am – F – C – G (vi – IV – I – V)
  • F – Fm – C – G (borrowed iv)

The lift: make the last chorus the biggest

  • Key change — move the final chorus up 1 or 2 semitones
  • New layer — harmony vocals, octave-up melody, a riff, bigger drums
  • Drop and return — first half of the chorus is just vocal + one instrument, then everything comes back
  • Ad-libs — the singer improvises around the melody

No bridge? A double final chorus with a lift works too.

Production tricks

Arrangement is subtraction. Something should change every 4 or 8 bars.

Pick at least three:

  • Drop-out — mute everything except the vocal (or drums) for 1–2 bars, then bring it back
  • Build / riser — snare roll, rising synth, or reverse cymbal before a chorus. Search Apple Loops for "riser," "sweep," or "uplifter"
  • Filter sweep — chords sound muffled, then open up
  • Volume automation — a pad fades in over the pre-chorus; the chorus gets a little louder
  • Ear candy — a tiny one-time sound: a "hey," a reversed note, a sound effect
  • Sampling — record a household sound in GarageBand's Sampler and play it as an instrument
  • Octave double — play the hook or riff an octave higher on a new sound in the chorus
  • Air pocket — one beat of silence right before the chorus

Transitions

At every section change, give a signal:

  • A drum fill
  • A crash cymbal on beat 1 of the new section
  • A riser
  • A drop-out
  • A one-beat stop

Reference check

Play your song, then a song you admire. Compare:

  • Is my vocal as clear?
  • Is my chorus as big?
  • Does something change as often?
